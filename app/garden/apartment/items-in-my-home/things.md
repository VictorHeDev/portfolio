## The things I optimized for

List the 3–5 priorities that explain most of your choices. For each, note any tradeoff you accepted.

- Priority: Comfort
  - Why it matters: I wanted a home that gave off the feeling of coziness as soon as you step through the doorway. I optimized this by having many small, globular, diffuse lighting spread across the apartment. My favorite lamps are a Herman Miller Nelson pendant lamp, the Lop lamp from a Vietnamese creator, and the infamous Ikea donut lamp. One of the age old adages is to spend a little extra care on what is between you and the ground, so I splurged a little for a Purple mattress and rugs (both secondhand) with cushiony rug pads underneath.
  - Tradeoff: It's impossible to turn on/off the lights without some automation, so I control these lights through various Apple Homepods strategically placed across the apartment. Buy once, cry once for the splurges that I expect to make a lasting difference.


- Priority: Space
  - Why it matters: I've had to make some compromises for the size of furniture I can keep in the apartment. This currently includes having a Daybed instead of a sectional, trying to maximize my vertical space to increase my walkable areas, and having a coffee table that can raise up instead of a dedicated dining table.
  - Tradeoff: Choosing smaller versions of items and oftentimes favoring function over design. The furniture that I have need to have dual purpose or a strong justification of why I'm keeping them. I've gotten a ton of use out of Ikea furniture like different sized Kallax (always with legs, to give the illusion of a taller apartment) and closed storage to hide clutter.


- Priority: Automation
  - Why it matters: I have a love-hate relationship with automation. I have a schedule for Schluter heated floors. I have a smaller-sized 18 inch Bosch dishwasher. And an Ubuntu home server that manages some Docker services including storage of important documents and a media server on my local network.
  - Tradeoff: How much of it is useful, and how much of it is tinkering for the sake of tinkering? I can't imagine having to manually turn on/off each one of my lights so I use a collection of Philips Hue, Kasa smart plugs, and Lutron light switches to control most of lights in the home.


## Living room

### [USM Haller R2]

I acquired this shelf from iconic Swiss-designed modular steel furniture created in 1963 by architect Fritz Haller and engineer Paul Schaerer. Commonly found in trendy coffee shops or high-end clothing stores, I found an amazing deal in Philadelphia. I drove out to meet the seller and we realized we both didn't have the proprietary tools to disassemble it. I ordered the tools from a German seller on Ebay and returned the next month, and drove it back to my previous apartment in Astoria.

The shelf lives partially assembled, which is one of the greatest perks of USM Haller pieces, their modularity. I'm using 2 drop-down leaf panels and 2 drawers as my TV console, and the rest of the shelfing is vertically stacked in my bedroom.

### [West elm coffee table]

Like I mentioned before, this coffee table is dual purpose. It has a top that rises up in case I want it for food and drinks, and there's convenient storage inside. This was also a Facebook Marketplace purchase and came with some minor scratches in the wood. Nothing a little elbow grease can't fix.

### [Ikea EVEDAL floor lamp]

I love how bulbous this lamp looks and its interesting dark, outer shade. I control it with a Philips Hue bulb. It is deceptively heavy.

### [Nelson Day Bed]

I got this for free on Facebook! I have it in a grey colorway. For now I can appreciate that it's comfortable, smartly sized for my living room, and there is space underneath for some storage bins. Ideally this will be an item I will trade or sell and upsize to a bigger couch. However this fits my needs for now because I'm still trying to figure out a better layout for the living room.

## Kitchen

My favorite thing about the kitchen are the natural wood cabinets contrasted with the sage green tiled backsplash. 

### [Viking stove]

My co-op uses gas lines so I found and got delivered a secondhand Viking stove from a Facebook Marketplace seller in SoHo. I degreased the oven and spent lots of time cleaning the nooks and crannies I could get into. The only issue I have with it is that sometimes the back right burner clicks when turning on one of the other burners. Apparently this can be caused by residue clogging up the igniter, but cleaning with isopropyl alcohol and a q-tip didn't fix it for me.

### [Stagg kettle]

I have a little Matcha and pourover coffee setup at home. The electric kettle helps me dial in the temperatures I need to get a decent brew. Whenever I travel I try to visit a coffee shop and buy some beans to brew at home. There's nothing better than spending several minutes brewing a fresh cup and the satisfaction of taking the first sip. It's a great reminder to slow down if I have time in the morning and to mentally prepare myself for the day.

### [Lazy susans]

Susan is anything but lazy. I use these for my fridge, narrow cabinets, and corner cabinets. They allow me to storage and reach items I otherwise would forget about and buy dupes of. And they're fun!

## Bathroom

### [Robern medicine cabinet]

I found this item on Facebook marketplace near Scarsdale, NY. I was looking specifically for a recessed medicine cabinet with a light fixture and got it for a steal. There's nothing wrong with it, but I do worry about the day one of the lights go out and I have to replace it.

### [Totto bidet]

This was a non-negotiable for me. It's true, once you use a bidet it's hard to ever go back. It also encourages using way less toilet paper so its likely paid for itself already. I bought the version with the controls connected to the seat, but there's a small part of me that wished I got the version where the controls are remote controlled/attached by an adhesive to the wall for a cleaner look.

### Toto Washlet A2

Out of all the items that I own, I think the Toto Bidet might be my favorite. She's not the prettiest, but I think she is the most functional and the item that I love using every day. I opted to get the model where the controls are on the side of the toilet to reduce the likelihood of failure. These bidets are bulletproof though, and so if I do have another chance, I might spring for the remote controlled on the wall model. I like this one because it has a heated, elongated seat and controls for water temperature.

Something that I found a little annoying though is the fact that I still need to turn my lights on in the middle of the night to use the restroom. I sort of wish that I splurged a little bit more to get the night light in the toilet.

### Schluter Ditra-Heat

When I redid my bathroom, I opted to get heated floors. I did this for a few reasons, one of which is to reduce the amount of humidity of the bathroom due to a the lack of ventilation. I thought that by heating the floors and the temperature difference of the ceiling of the bathroom would help circulate the air. I opted to go for tiled floors and the heated floors pair extremely well in the wintertime.
